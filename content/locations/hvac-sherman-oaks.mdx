Sherman Oaks summers run hot. Inland Valley conditions push afternoon temperatures above 100°F regularly from late June through September, and the housing stock — heavy on 1950s–60s ranch homes and post-war single-family — was built for a milder Valley than the one Sherman Oaks experiences now.

## What's Specific About HVAC in Sherman Oaks

The mid-century ranch homes that dominate Sherman Oaks were originally built without central AC. Most existing systems were retrofit installations done in the 1980s–2000s, and quality varied significantly. Common issues we see:

- **Undersized return air systems.** The original ductwork was sized for heating only. When AC was added, return air capacity often wasn't increased proportionally, causing high static pressure and reduced cooling output.
- **Aging R-22 systems.** A meaningful share of Sherman Oaks homes still operate on R-22 refrigerant systems installed in the late 1990s and 2000s. Refrigerant pricing for R-22 has climbed substantially — [leak repairs](/cost/refrigerant-leak-repair-cost-los-angeles) frequently push these systems into replacement territory.
- **Side-yard condenser placement.** Many Sherman Oaks homes have condensers tucked into narrow side yards with limited airflow clearance. This restricts heat rejection and accelerates [capacitor](/cost/ac-capacitor-replacement-cost-los-angeles) and motor wear.

## Sherman Oaks Microclimate

Sherman Oaks sits in a thermal pocket of the southern San Fernando Valley. Afternoon highs run 5–10°F above the Westside on identical days. The hottest stretch — typically late July through mid-September — regularly sees three to five 100°F+ heat events per summer.

For homeowners: this means your AC works harder for longer than systems in coastal LA. A capacitor that lasts 12 years in Santa Monica may last 7 in Sherman Oaks. A condenser coil that needs cleaning every two years on the coast needs annual cleaning here.
